Memento
Mori.

Live today as if it were your last day: five principles against procrastination.

From my book in progress, The Guide I Needed · Chapter 7

One day I was bored, watching Baraneemo, when I came across his podcast episodes. I was half-watching a video and half-listening to the podcast when I came across two words: Memento Mori. My eyes opened. What am I waiting for? Why am I standing still? Maybe today is my last day. And if I can’t do it, then what? Asking myself these questions made me realize something. That is why I will end this chapter with Baraneemo’s words.

Memento Mori is a Latin phrase. It means “remember that you will die.” That sounds dark, but for centuries people have used it as a reminder of something simple: your time is limited, so don’t waste it.

What It Changed for Me

Before, I carried my worries with me every single day. I thought about the same problems again and again, even when thinking about them changed nothing.

Memento Mori changed that. If today might be my last day, why spend it worrying about things I can’t control? So I started letting go of some of those worries and making more room for fun. I relaxed. And I started smiling more, which you will read about later in my book.

It didn’t make me lazy. It made me lighter. When you stop carrying every worry, you have more energy for the things that actually matter.

Dex

I used to have a cat named Dex. He always came to the door when we got home. When he died, I couldn’t believe it at first. It felt like he would be waiting at the door again the next time we came home.

Losing him reminded me what Memento Mori really means. It is not only about working harder or not wasting time. It is also about the people and animals you love. They will not be here forever, and neither will you. So love them now. Spend time with them now. Don’t be afraid to get attached just because one day you will lose them.

Live Today

Live today as if it were your last day. Even if the things you do feel boring, know how valuable they are. You will understand this better on the days when you feel stuck, like you are rowing a boat across the ocean.

Don’t let yesterday’s sadness take over today. Why would you give up today’s good for yesterday’s bad? Don’t wear yourself out with things that will never happen. Don’t replay that imaginary argument in your head for the twenty-fifth time; it only steals your energy.

And don’t say “tomorrow.” Saying “tomorrow” is easy, because your brain always looks for the path that costs the least energy. But if you put something off until tomorrow, you will probably put it off again tomorrow. The only day you can control is today.

Here are five principles that help me live this way:

  • Humility
  • Awareness
  • Meaning
  • Responsibility
  • Action

Your Exercise: What Matters Most?

Take a few minutes and answer these honestly.

  1. If today were your last day, what would you regret not doing?
  2. Which worry did you carry today that won’t matter in a year?
  3. Who do you want to spend more time with? When will you do it?
  4. What is one thing you will stop postponing, starting today?

Awareness Brings Control

“Awareness brings control.”

– Baraneemo

These words mean a lot to me. I first saw them on Baran’s Baraneemo channel. Being reminded that there is only today felt like watching procrastination die. I hung these words on my bedroom wall so I could see them every day. If you make them part of who you are, they can change the way you live each day. Finally:

Memento Mori.

This is a chapter from my book in progress, The Guide I Needed: A Teenager’s Guide to Finding Your Direction and Taking Action. The exercise questions are meant to be answered on paper.
