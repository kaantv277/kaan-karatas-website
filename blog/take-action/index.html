Seven small steps for your first week: the last step of my book, and the one that makes the others matter.

From my book in progress, The Guide I Needed · Chapter 15

If you have an idea, start. It doesn’t have to be perfect. First versions are usually rough, and you can only improve something once it exists. Writing your idea down is a good first step, but it is not the action itself. The action is the next thing you do with it.

This Is the Last Step

At the beginning of my book, I describe four steps: Understand → Choose → Build → Act. The chapters before this one are about your emotions and your worth, about what really matters to you, and about habits, persistence, relationships and boundaries.

Understanding yourself matters. But it only changes your life when you use it.

I know this from my own life. My archive didn’t start with a plan. It started with one video, one feeling and one decision to write things down. The first version was messy. But it existed, and you can only improve something that exists.

The same is true for emails. The three testimonies in my archive each began with one message to someone I had never met. Nothing would have happened if I had waited until I felt ready.

So I won’t give you another theory here. I’ll give you seven days.

Your First Seven Days

Day 1: Write down what you really want. Not what your family, your friends or social media want for you. What you want. One page, no editing.

Day 2: Find out what is stopping you. Fear? Time? Not knowing where to start? Other people’s opinions? Be honest.

Day 3: Choose one small habit. Something small enough that you can do it even on a bad day: ten minutes a day, one page a day, one email a day. If you miss a day, don’t blame yourself. Just start again the next day.

Day 4: Change your environment. Make the good habit easier and the bad one harder. Put the phone in another room. Put the book on your desk.

Day 5: Take one uncomfortable but reasonable step. Send the email. Ask the question. Sign up. This is your smart risk.

Day 6: Look back. What worked? What didn’t? What did you learn? Remember: a slow process is not a pointless one.

Day 7: Plan the next week. Keep what worked, change what didn’t, and choose your next step.

Your Exercise: Start Today

Write down your answers on paper.

  1. What is the one idea you will act on first?
  2. What will you do in the next 24 hours?
  3. Who could you tell once you have made real progress?

DON’T WAIT. START.
